Section 1

Introduction and Overview

1a. Purpose

The purpose of this guidebook is to familiarize providers with the culture, expectations, and policies that shape our work here at EMME Therapy Services.

Our main goal as a company is to ensure that every family has access to low cost, transformational therapy, and every provider enjoys unmatched compensation and quality of life.

Our Core Focus: Excellence for Everyone; a place where people and providers live their best life.
Our Niche: Transformational relationship-centered therapy that embraces others, empowers people, and extends empathy to all.

1b. Welcome

Welcome! We’re glad you’re here! EMME (aimer) is a French verb which means to like or to love. EMME is a mashup of values we hold: embracing others, empowering people, and extending empathy to all.

— Sara Beth Keppler and Jenny Riat, Owners

We opened EMME out of deep admiration and care for the brave, dynamic, and authentic families and providers that we meet each day.

EMME is a place that makes specialized care accessible to everyone. We believe everyone deserves to meet with an expert who believes in them and has the skills to help.

EMME is also a place where you can expect learning and growth from yourself and others. Our commitment to staff development, mentorship and quality of life means we’re always thinking about how to care for our staff to care for our community.

Working at EMME is the most fun job we’ve ever done. It is the honor of our career to think creatively about the systemic issues of our field for families and providers and build a future filled with mutual respect, possibility, and excellence for all.

1c. Overview of EMME Therapy Services

At EMME, we believe that client and practitioner satisfaction are essential to our success as an organization and community.

We’ve hired you because we have high standards of practice, and we know you can meet them. Excellence is our expectation. It is a privilege to work at EMME, and we each have a unique responsibility to be worthy of that privilege.

We expect, above all, for every provider to conduct themselves in a way that is respectful to themselves and others. Our policies in this guidebook are designed to uphold the highest standard of practice and protect the integrity of care for the clients served.

Key concepts we believe in

Excellence

Holding high expectations, equipped with the resources to meet and maintain them. Excellence at EMME is for both providers and families. This is why we write grants for continuing ed, provide strong training and mentorship programs, plan free programming for families, and encourage flexibility for providers to be human and therapists.

Integrity

Being accountable to what you know to be true, while staying connected to yourself and others. “[Being trustworthy] means being reliable, doing what we say we will to the best of our ability, and honoring relationships where trust is being built with care and communication.” — Prentis Hemphill

Imagination

The joy, humor and possibility of “what if?” We apply the creativity of the therapeutic process to the systemic issues in our field for providers, practices, and families.

Community

Becoming a more just, compassionate, and cooperative world. “…politics is simply how we make decisions about the resources we have. Who we decide is excluded and on what basis. It’s our actions and what we take responsibility for. It’s what we choose to do about the messes history has left us. In a time when especially national politics, big-P politics, can feel so disempowering, overwhelming, looking more deeply at what’s around each of us — what political decisions and actions we can make, what pressure our community can apply, what new way we can dream up — gives us the opportunity to feel the agency available to us in the risk of taking action and building power. It’s an opportunity to demand more from the world and create more in it.”

Reflection

Asking “why?” without judgement of ourselves or others. “Emotional honesty really is the ability to tell the truth about yourself. And that means that you, first of all, have to look for the truth about yourself.” — Dr. Galit Atlas. We engage in reflective practice to examine the patterns and beliefs we hold and how those support or impede our work and self-wellness in the workplace.

1d. Changes in policy

The employee guidebook is updated annually and on an ad-hoc basis. It is not an exhaustive list of all policies and procedures but a highlight of some of the most important ones. Policies stated in the current version of the guidebook supersede the content of documents from prior years. Please reach out to jenriat@emmeco.org with any feedback or suggestions.

General Employment Information

2a. Payroll schedule

Providers are paid monthly via direct deposit. Payroll runs during the last week of the month for the previous month’s sessions. For example, sessions held 8/1–8/31 are paid 9/25.

Sample onboarding timeline and payroll

  • 8/1–8/14: Provider completes trainings and contractor to-do list
  • 8/15–8/24: Provider meets with Jenny for an onboarding meeting and starts to accept referrals. Provider works with SB to complete agency orientations and attend the universal training.
  • 8/25–8/31: first visit(s) with families
  • 9/25–9/31: Payroll for 8/1–8/31

2b. Compensation and benefits

Provider rates are determined by the contract agreement. EMME Therapy Services pays providers on a salary basis (based on a productivity requirement), or per unit of intervention administered (based on a pay per visit model).

2026 EMME Salary Schedule and Compensation Plan

Providers document their hours toward productivity in the IntakeQ practice portal on the BOOKINGS calendar. Providers must complete, sign and lock a note for each session. Medical providers (SLP, OT, PT) servicing Medicaid clients are also required to clock hours in the Sandata EVV app. See section 3g for Sandata requirements. See section 3e for documentation in IntakeQ.

2c. Tax withholdings

For all employees, EMME Therapy Services pays half of Social Security and Medicare (FICA) taxes, equaling 7.65 percent. EMME Therapy Services withholds the other 7.65 percent from employee paychecks each pay period.

For all employees, EMME Therapy Services pays half of the Colorado FAMLI Leave taxes, equaling 0.44 percent. EMME Therapy Services withholds the other 0.44 percent from employee paychecks each pay period.

The amount of income tax withheld on a paycheck varies based on how the provider completes their tax withholding information. Contact jenriat@emmeco.org for more information about the current percentage of tax withholdings on an individual’s check.

2d. Mileage

The company reimburses providers for mileage for all clients at the rate of $0.67 per mile. See Provider Responsibilities 3c, Mileage, Maps, and Hybrid/In Person Services, for more information.

2e. Benefits

Part-time and full-time employees are eligible for health, dental, and vision benefits. EMME Therapy Services will pay up to $180.00 monthly toward the cost of an employee’s benefits plan. Health, dental, and vision benefits are completely customized for each employee. The remainder of an employee’s benefits monthly premium will be withheld from the paycheck.

Contact jenriat@emmeco.org to get in touch with our benefits specialist.

2f. Expenses and reimbursements

Providers are eligible for paid training or professional development through grant funds. All reimbursements for training exist for the duration of the grant, only. Contact jenriat@emmeco.org for registration information. If a provider registers for a training without approval, reimbursement cannot be guaranteed.

2g. Remote work

All providers are eligible to work remotely, should they choose. Any provider who provides telehealth services must use a secure, HIPAA-compliant video conferencing software that allows for group calls with 3 or more providers. Providers must have a high-speed internet connection, appropriate background, and clear camera. If a family is 5–10 minutes from a provider’s home, services should be provided hybrid if at all possible.

Setting Up Telehealth for Success

2h. Time off

EMME Therapy Services provides 4 weeks of PTO at the provider’s salaried productivity rate (if productivity is 25 hours per week, 1 week of PTO is 25 hours). PTO is accrued at 1 week per quarter. For providers who are pay-per visit, they accrue 1 hour of PTO for every 30 hours worked, up to 48 hours per year. The leave policy is compliant with the Colorado Healthy Families and Workplaces Act (HFWA).

Providers also have access to the Colorado FAMLI program, which ensures all Colorado workers have access to paid leave in order to take care of themselves or their family during life circumstances that pull them away from their jobs.

Federal FMLA applies to businesses who have at least 50 employees. The state of Colorado follows federal guidelines for FMLA. EMME Therapy Services is not currently covered under FMLA. See the Colorado notices for more information.

2i. Leave practices

Providers are given a great deal of autonomy over their schedules. If a provider wishes to take time off without taking PTO, the provider is responsible for rescheduling visits to meet the productivity requirement listed in the contract for the quarter.

If a provider wishes to take extended time off (more than 2 weeks), contact jenriat@emmeco.org at least 1 month in advance to coordinate with a substitute service provider.

Foreseeable leave: In general, the employee must give the employer at least 30 days advance notice of the need to take leave when he or she knows about the need for the leave in advance, and it is possible and practical to do so.

Unforeseeable leave: When the need for leave is unexpected, the employee must provide notice to the employer as soon as possible and practical.

Leave payment: If a provider does not have PTO accrued, leave will be unpaid.

If a provider wishes to use PTO, they should create an appointment for themselves in IntakeQ with the following details:

  • Client Name: Provider
  • Location: EMME
  • Service: PTO Leave
  • Duration: Number of PTO hours in MINUTES

2j. Inclement weather policy

If the school district where the provider or family resides delays or closes school, any in person visits will be moved to virtual for the duration of the closure or delay.

Provider Responsibilities

3a. Trainings, orientations, and meet and greets

All providers are responsible for completing required state trainings (Provider Training, Team training), and attending the GO4IT training and any required agency orientations or meet and greets. Access information on agency-specific requirements.

3b. Productivity, schedule, and cancellations

Productivity

The minimum number of visits required for any staff member is 6 hours per week.

Providers need to have 1.25 × their planned productivity open on their schedule (a productivity of 25 has 32 open slots).

Productivity is determined by the number of client-facing hours worked per week (a 30-minute visit is 0.5 hours, a 90-minute visit is 1.5 hours).

How to calculate productivity: number of weeks in a quarter × your total number of sessions in a week.

Example: There are 13.2 weeks in Quarter 3, so the total number of sessions should be your weekly productivity × 13.2 (25 × 13.2 = 330).

You are responsible for monitoring your productivity. Please check this weekly. Example: Your productivity is 25. If you see 24 hours one week, the next week should have 26 hours.

If a provider on salary does not meet productivity for 2 months in a row, Emme will modify the productivity and salary.

Schedule

The EMME Schedule Template is available here.

Providers are responsible for keeping their schedule template up-to-date on a weekly basis.

Providers are required to notify administration of upcoming openings at least 30 days prior to the opening.

Cancellations

The EMME Cancellation Policy is linked here. Every family reviews this document and signs it during their onboarding call.

Providers are expected to monitor their cancellation rates. Reschedules and Practitioner Cancels are not included in a provider’s cancellation rate.

Cancellation rate = (Late Cancels + Client Cancels) ÷ (Confirmed Visits + Late Cancels and Client Cancels)
  • Under 10%: Excellent
  • 10–15%: Healthy / pretty typical
  • 15–20%: Worth monitoring
  • 20%+: Review

If a provider has a consistently high cancellation rate and families are declining to reschedule, management will sit in on sessions to support the provider gaining the skills to hold consistent sessions.

Directions for documenting cancellations accurately in IntakeQ are linked here. A provider should never leave a cancellation reason blank.

3c. Mileage, maps, and hybrid and in-person services

Mileage

EMME reimburses providers at the company rate of $0.67 per mile for work-related driving based on the following scenarios:

  • Driving from the provider’s home to a family’s service location.
  • Driving from a family’s service location to another family’s service location.
  • Driving from a family’s service location to the provider’s home.

In order to be reimbursed for mileage, providers are required to submit mileage by the last day of the month. The template for mileage tracking can be found here.

Maps

Each provider has a Google map with their in-person area. It is linked to the second page of their schedule template.

Providers are required to keep their map up to date with clients who have been added or discharged.

Hybrid and in-person services

Providers are expected to provide hybrid and in-person services at least 1× monthly if someone resides within their in-person area.

Providers are assigned an in-person area for services and are not reimbursed for mileage driven outside of their in-person area.

Providers are not required to provide services in person at a frequency higher than what was assigned during the intake call.

3d. Interpreters and translation

All providers are expected to collaborate with interpreters to service families. The referrals team sets up interpreters for initial and ongoing visits at intake. If you need support scheduling an interpreter outside of a regular visit, contact referrals@emmeco.org.

Providers are expected to share next steps for families who speak another language in their native language. Best practice is asking the interpreter in the last few moments of the session to translate the next steps so that it can be shared with the family.

  • Service: This is the service provided. Sometimes change this. When to change the service.
  • Duration: This is the length of the visit. Always change this to reflect the time you met.
  • Practitioner note: Always add a note if there is a split visit. See when to change the service. Always add a discharge note if it is the final visit. See information on discharge notes and the discharge example.

3g. Sandata (medical providers)

All medical providers (SLP, OT, PT) must use the Sandata Electronic Visit Verification (EVV) app to log in and out of Medicaid sessions. Directions for using Sandata.

3h. Co-treatment policies

Co-treatment sessions between two providers are a covered service under the following conditions:

  • Valid clinical rationale for providing co-treatment must be present. Providers should refer to the Joint Guidelines for Therapy Co-Treatment developed by ASHA, AOTA, and APTA.
  • Each provider must have an approved Plan of Care (or IFSP for Early Intervention) which includes co-treatment.
  • Each provider must have an approved Prior Authorization which includes the Plan of Care or IFSP documentation that co-treatment will be used.

Medicaid doesn’t reimburse the same way as the state when it comes to co-treatments. As such, we can only hold co-treatments when state funding is involved. We cannot bill Medicaid for a co-treat between 2 Medicaid providers (for example, SLP and OT).

Billable co-treatments include:

  • Medicaid SLP and DI/SE/CHIP
  • Medicaid OT and DI/SE/CHIP
  • Medicaid PT and DI/SE/CHIP
  • State-funded SLP/OT/PT and DI/SE/CHIP

In special circumstances, we can receive permission to co-treat between 2 Medicaid providers. In these situations, contact administration to confirm billing approval.

Co-treatment practices:

  • Co-treatment is appropriate when coordination between the two disciplines will benefit the patient, not simply for scheduling convenience.
  • Documentation should clearly indicate the rationale for co-treatment and state the goals that will be addressed through this method of intervention.
  • Co-treatment sessions should be documented as such by each practitioner, stating which goals were addressed and the progress made.
  • Co-treatment should be limited to two disciplines providing interventions during one treatment session.

3l. Information security

Providers are expected to comply with practice information security measures outlined in the Emme Co HIPAA Plan and BAA.

  • All devices (desktop, laptop, iPad, phone) should be password protected.
  • Any printers or external devices that may store protected health information (PHI) need to be cleared so as to not save PHI inadvertently.
  • Any devices in which PHI is downloaded need to delete the PHI after use and delete it from recycling (true deletion).
  • Providers should log out of the computer after ending a work session. Devices should be set to automatically log out after 5 minutes of inactivity.
  • If a provider suspects a breach of information security, notify Jenny and SB immediately.
  • Documents containing PHI should only be saved in cloud-based software that is HIPAA-compliant (PracticeQ, Microsoft OneDrive).
  • Documents containing PHI should not be saved or stored locally. Do not save documents containing PHI to Google Drive or iCloud.
  • Computers and cloud-based software should not be checked to “remember me” to avoid additional logins or multi-factor authentication.
  • All requests for records or files should run through Jenny and SB.
  • Providers should install workstations with regular updates of anti-virus and other protective software as appropriate for the device.

3m. Provider offboarding

When a provider leaves the practice, the practice will handle the transition of care for all clients. This includes notifying service coordinators, families, and payer partners.

All clients are served through the agency. They are not the sole responsibility of any individual provider. Individual practitioner-family relationships are important. And all communication regarding transition practices must remain aligned with agency policies and professional ethical standards. There is a lot that occurs in the background at a practice level that an individual provider may not be aware of.

Staff should not directly or indirectly encourage, guide, or facilitate families to leave the practice or make changes to services without including leadership. This includes informal suggestions, sharing external opportunities, or positioning services in a way that influences a family’s decision to leave. If a family independently expresses interest in changing providers or agencies, or a family has questions about their options, this must be communicated to leadership. Allowing leadership to handle these conversations ensures transitions can be handled appropriately and with continuity of care in mind. It is our job to make sure private health information is handled appropriately, and we have a responsibility to present families with all options available to them.

Any actions that compromise these expectations will be reviewed and addressed accordingly.

Our priority is to protect the integrity of our services and ensure families receive consistent, ethical care. Please reach out with any questions.

Texas Workplace Policies

Texas English labor notices Federal English labor notices Texas Labor Code

5a. Crime victim leave

See the Texas labor notices linked above.

5b. Day of rest law

A person who is an employer may not require an employee to work seven consecutive days in an establishment, the business of which is selling merchandise at retail.

The person may not deny an employee at least one period of 24 consecutive hours of time off for rest or worship in each seven-day period. The time off must be in addition to the regular periods of rest allowed during each day worked.

The person shall accommodate the religious beliefs and practices of an employee unless the employer can demonstrate that to do so would constitute an undue hardship on the conduct of the employer’s business. In addition, the person may not require an employee to work during a period that the employee requests to be off to attend one regular worship service a week of the employee’s religion.

This section does not apply to employment of a part-time employee whose total work hours for one employer during a calendar week do not exceed 30 hours.

5c. Jury duty leave

Jury duty leave is job-protected leave. An employee who is on jury duty is entitled to protection against termination or other adverse action by the employer (see §§ 122.001 and 122.0022 of the Juror’s Right to Reemployment Act in the Texas Civil Practices and Remedies Code). Paid leave for jury duty is not required.

5d. Voting leave

Assuming that an employee has not already voted in early voting, the employee is entitled to take paid time off for voting on election days, unless the employee has at least two consecutive hours to vote outside of the voter’s working hours.

5e. Witness duty and court appearance leave

Just as with military leave and leave to serve as a subpoenaed witness in a court or administrative proceeding, an employer should not count jury duty leave toward an absence limit.
